General Guidelines

Things that make for a good paper are: clear and accurate reconstructions of claims and arguments from the readings and lecture handouts, a clearly articulated position, reasons for taking that position, answers to likely criticisms, and evidence of thinking the issue through rather than repeating a Zen slogan. Historical papers should distinguish what a source says from what its date, genre, and institutional setting allow it to establish.

All writing must be your own. Put exact wording in quotation marks and identify its source. You may quote a handout, but the bulk of the paper should be in your own wording. Whenever another scholar's interpretation, a translator's decision, or a distinctive argument appears in your paper, credit the source. If a claim relies on a late record about an earlier master, say so rather than silently presenting the record as a transcript.
